## Run 1, at 1175b2ef, went RED twice and the ladder was not wrong either time

Recorded because a suppressed first run would have made the second look easier
than it was. Started 22:39, finished 23:13, `RUN_STATUS=RED`:

- `bm802_pin_bitest` was declared a negative leg and marked expect-non-zero. It is
  not one: it prints `[BITES]` and exits 0 when the AssertionError fires, and exits
  1 only if a wrong pin slips through. The driver's own comment inverted its
  verdict. The mechanism has since been deleted rather than corrected -- no leg in
  the list needs an expected-sign column.
- `bm653` exited 1 at 554 s with `run_bm653_e2e.sh: line 155: echo: write error:
  No space left on device`, in its regression leg, on the filesystem that held the
  scratch tree (2.5 GB free at the start of that leg). Every boot leg before it had
  gone through: `IDENTITY: GREEN 49 RED 0`, seven boots, the fork's size leg. So the
  box ran out, not the ladder.

Both are now the driver's business: it refuses to start under 4 GB on the scratch
device, says the device and the free space in its opening line, scans each leg's log
for the phrase and reports `ENV-FAIL` rather than `FAIL` when it finds it, and the
scratch tree for run 2 was placed on a filesystem with room.

## The pristine tree did not stay pristine, and the six files it moved say something

The driver now re-archives the same commit after the legs and diffs the two trees;
at the run recorded above that step was done by hand, with the same command,
against this run's scratch tree, and re-running it after the script grew the step
returned exactly these six.

    rung6/bm602_fixtures.json
    rung6_5/evidence/bm653/e2e_pass_1.txt
    rung6_5/evidence/bm653/e2e_pass_2.txt
    rung6_5/evidence/bm653/transcripts/exec_green.serial
    rung6_5/evidence/bm653/transcripts/exec_image_repaired.serial
    rung9/bm902_diff_receipt.txt

Every changed line is a duration, a spinner, or a timestamp: `bm602_fixtures.json`
holds two `*_seconds` fields, the two `e2e_pass_*.txt` hold the per-leg `Ns` column
(16 lines each, e.g. `p1_s4_mkimg rc=0 20s` -> `25s`), the two `.serial` transcripts
differ in how many `-`/`\`/`|` spinner characters the boot emitted, and the BM902
receipt moved its own `date:` from 21:58:21Z to 23:27:50Z. No verdict, no sha pin,
no fault offset and no gate value differs -- the byte sizes of all six files are
unchanged but for `e2e_pass_2.txt` (2 B) and the two transcripts (37 B and 12 B).

That is the finding, and it generalises the one ROADMAP's TASK_BM001 bullet already
carries about the working tree: **running the ladder's own gates is what makes a
tree dirty, in six tracked files, and it does so in a checkout that was pristine a
minute earlier.** A reviewer who lands the tree and then checks `git status` to see
whether the landing worked will see dirt that is not a mistake, and BM653's gate
overwrites its own landed evidence under `rung6_5/evidence/` while doing it.

## The bounded BM802 preflight, read against the landed 363 rows

`rung8/bm802_sweep_rows.txt` (363 boots, landed) holds a row for each of the 12
ids the preflight draws, so the comparison is leg-for-leg rather than by count. On
`verdict`, `stage`, `region`, `kind`, `gate`, `guest_crc` and `prompt`, 11 of the 12
match their landed row exactly. The twelfth, `width-stage2-code`, repeats its
verdict `INERT` and lands at stage 5 with no shell prompt where the landed row
reached stage 6 -- which is exactly the divergence
`rung8/bm802_pristine_preflight.txt` already documents for this row between the
landed rig and a pristine tree, reproduced here rather than newly introduced.
`bytes` (transcript length) differs on 7 of the 12 and is not a comparable field:
the same boot emits a different number of spinner characters each time.
